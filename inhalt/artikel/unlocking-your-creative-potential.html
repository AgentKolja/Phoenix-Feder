Think about it - when do your best ideas come to you?

It’s when you make space for creativity to appear.

“Create space to let space create.”

This is the heart of mindful creativity.

Creativity thrives on openness, curiosity, and trust in the process.

For years, I unknowingly blocked creativity by trying too hard, overthinking, or constantly distracting myself with small tasks. I’ve since learned that filling every moment with mindless activity stifles your ability to create.

Three Mindful Strategies to unlock your creative potential

1. Trust your Subconscious Mind

Your subconscious is a powerful creative ally.

When you give it a question or focus point and let it process in the background, it often delivers fresh insights.

  • Before sleep or a nap: Ask yourself a question that you are searching the answer to: “Will I be happy?” or “How can I fascinate my audience?” Then let it go. The time right before and after sleep is the most influential on your subconscious. Answers will emerge naturally.

  • Create Space: When creativity feels stuck, stepping away can make all the difference. Take a walk, meditate, or engage in a calming activity—these intentional pauses recharge your mind and often lead to clarity or fresh insights.

  • Stop disctractions: Instead of distracting yourself with your phone or unnecessary tasks, let your mind wander freely between activities. These quiet moments often spark unexpected ideas and connections.

2. Mind-Friendly Thinking

Our brains love structure and patterns - that makes acrostics such a powerful tool for organizing and internalizing ideas. Here’s how you can use acrostics effectively in learning and training contexts:

  • To kick off a new topic: E.g. in a vision workshop, use the company name as an acrostic to structure brainstorming. For example, PORSCHE could stand for Passion, Opportunity, Reach, Strategy, Creativity, Hope, Execution. This energizes participants and provides direction for their ideas.

  • To internalize knowledge: At the end of a training, invite participants to create their own acrostic for the topic. For instance, in a coaching workshop, they could use IDENTITY to personalize and summarize what they’ve learned about themselves. This helps them solidify key takeaways in a way that resonates individually.

3. Break Routine Thinking

Which rhetorical method is the very essence of an invitation? A question!

Here are two approaches for questions that boost creativity:

  • Ask Contrarian Questions: Flip the script by asking the opposite of what you’d usually focus on. For example: How could we make this even worse?

  • Use open ended sentences: Start a sentence and leave it open-ended for yourself or your conversation partner to complete. Try these examples:

    • The world would be a better place if…

    • Self-esteem depends mostly on…

    • I’d have fewer problems if…

Mindfulness is the Essence of Creativity

At its core, there is no creativity without mindfulness. Every original idea begins with presence.

When you let go of mindlessness you create the space creativity needs to thrive.

“Create space to let space create.”

The next time you face a creative challenge, try this mindful approach:

  • Plant a seed in your subconscious by asking a question before sleep or a break.

  • Take reflective pauses to recharge.

  • Start asking unusual questions!

Invite creativity instead of forcing it. Mindfulness is the key to making that happen.

Now’s the time to try something new.
